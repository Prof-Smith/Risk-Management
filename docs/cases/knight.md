# Knight Capital — Trading Glitch and Operational Failure

---

## Overview

The Knight Capital trading glitch represents one of the most significant real-world failures of operational and model risk in modern financial markets.

On August 1, 2012, Knight deployed new trading software that unintentionally activated dormant test code, generating millions of erroneous orders across more than 150 stocks. Within 45 minutes, the firm accumulated approximately $7 billion in unintended positions and ultimately incurred losses of around $440 million during liquidation. 

This case examines how failures in software deployment, model governance, and operational controls can combine to create firm-threatening losses.

## Analytical Lens

**Insight:** Operational failures in automated systems can propagate rapidly and create catastrophic financial losses within minutes.

**Quant Takeaway:** Knight accumulated ~$7B in unintended positions, with modeled losses of ~$420M–$440M driven by combined price impact during forced liquidation. 

**Model Implication:** Effective risk management requires strong governance, change control, independent validation, and real-time monitoring in systems operating at high speed.
