# Global Financial Crisis (2008)

---

## Overview

The 2008 Global Financial Crisis demonstrates how multiple risk systems—credit, market, liquidity, and counterparty—can fail simultaneously.

Financial institutions relied on:

- historical default data  
- credit ratings  
- diversification assumptions  

These inputs proved unreliable as housing prices declined and correlations increased.

---

## Analytical Focus

- Market-implied vs historical risk (CDS vs default rates)  
- Structural credit analysis (Altman Z)  
- Portfolio loss modeling (Credit VaR)  
- Counterparty exposure (CVA)  
- Wrong-way risk and downgrade triggers  

---

## Analytical Lens

**Insight:** Systemic crises occur when independent risk models fail simultaneously.

**Quant Takeaway:** CDS-implied risk, structural credit metrics, and portfolio loss models diverge significantly under stress, signaling model breakdown.

**Model Implication:** Risk management must integrate multiple models and account for interaction effects across credit, market, and liquidity risk.

---

## Position Within Risk Framework

- LTCM → model failure  
- Lehman → liquidity failure  
- London Whale → measurement failure  
- **GFC → systemic interaction failure**

---

## Key Lesson

> Financial crises are driven not by a single failure, but by the interaction of multiple risk systems.
