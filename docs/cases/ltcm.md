# Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM)

LTCM provides a canonical example of model-driven risk failure.

However, rather than focusing only on the narrative, this case examines LTCM through a **quantitative risk framework using yield curve data (1997–1998)**.

---

## Why This Case Matters

LTCM did not fail because markets were random.

It failed because:
- Risk models were calibrated to "normal" conditions
- Volatility and correlation were assumed stable
- Large, systemic shocks were not incorporated properly

This case explores how those assumptions break down.

---

## Analytical Focus

In this lab, you will analyze LTCM through:

- **Volatility estimation**
  - Rolling vs EWMA volatility
  - Regime shifts during the crisis

- **Yield curve factor risk**
  - Principal components (Shift, Twist, Curvature)
  - Systematic drivers of interest rate movements

- **Regime-dependent risk**
  - Differences between pre-crisis and crisis environments
  - Changes in volatility and correlation

- **Value at Risk (VaR)**
  - Risk estimates under normal conditions
  - Comparison across base vs stress regimes

---

## Key Learning Insight

This case highlights a central principle of risk management:

> Models work well when their assumptions hold —  
> but become dangerous when those assumptions are violated.
