# Global Financial Crisis (2008) — Systemic Risk Interaction

---

## Executive Summary

The Global Financial Crisis demonstrates how multiple risk models can fail simultaneously, leading to systemic instability.

While individual models remained internally consistent, their combined interactions—across credit risk, market pricing, liquidity, and counterparty exposure—produced outcomes far beyond expected losses.

---

## Core Problem

- Reliance on historical default data and ratings  
- Underestimation of correlation and tail dependence  
- Hidden counterparty exposure (AIG)  
- Weak treatment of liquidity and collateral dynamics  

---

## Analytical Approach

This case integrates:

- CDS-implied default probabilities  
- Altman Z-score (firm-level credit health)  
- Credit VaR (portfolio loss modeling)  
- CVA (counterparty exposure)  
- Wrong-way risk under stress  

---

## Key Insight

> The most severe financial crises occur when independent risk models become jointly wrong.

---

## Implication for Risk Management

- Models must be evaluated jointly, not in isolation  
- Correlation and systemic linkage must be stress-tested  
- Liquidity and collateral frameworks must be integrated  
- Counterparty risk must account for wrong-way exposure  

---

## Position Within Risk Framework

-	LTCM → Model failure
-	Lehman → Liquidity failure
-	London Whale → Measurement failure
-	GFC → Systemic interaction
