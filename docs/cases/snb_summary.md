# Sunrise National Bank (SNB) — Stress Testing and Scenario Analysis

---

## Executive Summary

The SNB case illustrates how forward-looking stress testing reveals vulnerabilities that remain hidden under normal conditions.

While the bank appears adequately capitalized in baseline scenarios, stress testing exposes significant deterioration driven by credit losses, market shocks, and liquidity stress.

---

## Core Problem

- Reliance on baseline capital ratios  
- Underestimation of stress interactions  
- Insufficient integration of liquidity and market risks  
- Limited visibility into extreme scenarios  

---

## Analytical Approach

The case evaluates:

- Multiple stress scenarios (CRE collapse, supervisory adverse, reverse stress)  
- Loss composition across portfolios  
- CET1 capital deterioration  
- Reverse stress testing to identify viability thresholds  

---

## Key Findings

- CET1 declines sharply under stress (~4.8%, ~3.1%, ~4.3%) [1](https://emailsaintleo-my.sharepoint.com/personal/zachary_smith_saintleo_edu/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/BaselIII_Full_Plotly_Student_Notebook%20(1)%20(2).ipynb%20-%20Colab.pdf)  
- Supervisory scenario breaches regulatory capital requirements [1](https://emailsaintleo-my.sharepoint.com/personal/zachary_smith_saintleo_edu/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/BaselIII_Full_Plotly_Student_Notebook%20(1)%20(2).ipynb%20-%20Colab.pdf)  
- Credit losses dominate total losses  
- Liquidity stress and counterparty exposure amplify impacts  

---

## Key Insight

> Stress testing reveals risks that are invisible in baseline or historical models.

---

## Implication for Risk Management

- Firms must adopt forward-looking stress testing frameworks  
- Capital adequacy must be evaluated under extreme conditions  
- Liquidity risk must be integrated with credit and market risk  
- Reverse stress testing should identify failure thresholds proactively
