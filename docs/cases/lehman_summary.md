# Lehman Brothers (2008) — Liquidity and Confidence Failure

---

## Executive Summary

Lehman Brothers’ collapse demonstrates that strong capital ratios do not guarantee survival in financial markets.

Despite reporting Tier 1 capital levels comparable to its peers, the firm failed within days due to a rapid loss of market confidence and funding access.

---

## Core Problem

The central issue was not balance sheet insolvency, but:

- **Dependence on short-term funding markets**
- **Uncertainty around asset valuation**
- **Erosion of counterparty confidence**
- **Inability to secure liquidity under stress**

These factors triggered a classic institutional “run” dynamic.

---

## Analytical Approach

The analysis evaluates:

- Leverage trends and capital proxy (equity-to-assets)  
- Comparison between Tier 1 capital and balance sheet-based measures  
- Timeline of key events leading to failure  
- Interaction between liquidity, solvency, and market perception  

---

## Key Findings

- Reported Tier 1 capital remained relatively stable (~11%) prior to collapse  
- Simple capital measures suggested vulnerability not reflected in regulatory metrics  
- Funding pressures escalated rapidly as confidence deteriorated  
- Market participants disregarded capital ratios during the crisis  

---

## Key Insight

> Financial institutions fail when they lose funding access —  
> not necessarily when they first become insolvent.

---

## Implications for Risk Management

- Liquidity risk must be evaluated alongside capital adequacy  
- Stress testing should incorporate funding withdrawal scenarios  
- Balance sheet transparency is critical to maintaining confidence  
- Capital measures alone are insufficient indicators of resilience  

---
