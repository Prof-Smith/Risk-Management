# JPMorgan London Whale (2012)

---

## Case Overview

The JPMorgan London Whale incident highlights failures in market risk measurement and internal risk controls.

In this case, large credit derivative positions produced losses exceeding $6 billion. These losses exposed weaknesses in:

- Value-at-Risk (VaR) models  
- Tail risk estimation  
- Oversight and governance  

---

## Why This Case Matters

Unlike:

- **LTCM** → model assumptions failed  
- **Lehman** → liquidity and confidence collapsed  

This case shows:

> Risk models can appear correct but still fail under extreme conditions.

---

## Analytical Focus

In this lab, you will analyze:

- VaR vs Expected Shortfall (ES)  
- Risk behavior under normal vs stress scenarios  
- Visualization of tail risk and model failure  
- Governance and oversight breakdown  

---

## Key Learning Insight

> Measuring risk incorrectly can be as dangerous as ignoring risk altogether.
