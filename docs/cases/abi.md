# Atlas Bank International (ABI)

---

## Overview

The Atlas Bank International (ABI) case examines how global banking regulation evolved from Basel I to Basel III and how those changes transformed bank risk profiles, capital structures, and strategic decisions.

ABI is a large, globally active bank with significant exposure to structured credit, trading activities, and short-term funding markets.

Before the financial crisis, ABI relied heavily on:

- Proprietary trading  
- Structured credit positions (CDOs)  
- Short-term repo funding  
- Off-balance sheet exposures 

This case follows ABI through:

- Regulatory arbitrage under Basel I  
- Model-driven risk under Basel II  
- Crisis-driven failures  
- Regulatory reforms (Basel II.5 and Basel III)  
- Strategic transformation  

---

## Analytical Focus

- Evolution of Basel I → II → II.5 → III  
- Model risk and regulatory arbitrage  
- Trading-book failures and VaR limitations  
- Capital adequacy and liquidity requirements  
- Strategic responses by bank leadership  

---

## Analytical Lens

**Insight:** Changes in regulation fundamentally reshape how risk is measured, capitalized, and managed within financial institutions.

**Quant Takeaway:** ABI’s VaR (~$35–42M pre-crisis) dramatically understated realized losses (~$200M to ~$4.5B), revealing severe model failure and insufficient capital under Basel II.

**Model Implication:** Effective risk management requires robust capital, stressed risk measures, and liquidity constraints, rather than reliance on internal models calibrated to stable periods.
